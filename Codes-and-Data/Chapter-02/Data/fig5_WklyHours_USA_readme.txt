Figure 5: Average weekly hours worked per working age population in the U.S. for 1900-2024.
Sources:
(a) For years 1900-2005: Francis and Ramey (2009) 
https://econweb.ucsd.edu/~vramey/research/Century_Public_Data.xls 
(b) For years 2006-2024: Data for 2006-2024 is constructed using three data sources following Francis and Ramey (2009): 
Series 1: "All employees, thousands, total private, seasonally adjusted," from BLS Current Employment Statistics (CES) variable CES0500000001 
https://data.bls.gov/dataViewer/view/timeseries/CES0500000001 
Series 2: "Hours usually worked per week at all jobs," from Census Bureau Current Population Survey (CPS) 
CPS-IPUMS, variable UHRSWORKT, weighted by ASECWTH. Observations with NaN and top-coded values are excluded. 
Series 3: "Working-Age Population Total: From 15 to 64 Years for U.S.," from FRED variable LFWA64TTUSM647S 
https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/LFWA64TTUSM647S 
First, I calculated the annual weighted average of the weekly hours worked per employee using the CPS data. 
Second, I multiplied this average by the total number of employees from the CES data, to obtain a measure of total weekly hours worked by all employees. 
Third and finally, I divided the total weekly hours worked by the working-age population from the FRED data to obtain the average weekly hours worked per working-age person.
